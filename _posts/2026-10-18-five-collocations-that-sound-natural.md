A **collocation** is a group of words that naturally go together. English speakers say *heavy rain* and *make a decision*. They don't say *strong rain* or *do a decision*, even though you would be understood. Choosing the right partner word is one of the quickest ways to sound more natural.

## 1. Make or do?

| make | do |
| --- | --- |
| make a decision | do homework |
| make a mistake | do the dishes |
| make progress | do your best |
| make an effort | do research |

*Make* is often about creating or producing something, and *do* is often about tasks and activities. That is only a tendency, so learn the phrases as whole units.

## 2. Heavy or strong?

- **heavy**: heavy rain, heavy traffic, a heavy smoker
- **strong**: strong coffee, strong wind, a strong accent

## 3. Take a...

*take a break*, *take a photo*, *take a chance*, *take your time*

## 4. Pay attention

We *pay* attention, not *give* it. Other natural partners: *pay a compliment*, *pay a visit*.

## 5. Fast or quick?

- **fast** describes speed: a fast car, a fast train, fast internet
- **quick** describes something that takes a short time: a quick look, a quick question, a quick shower
- Some pairs are fixed: *fast food*

## How to learn collocations

1. **Learn chunks, not single words.** When you meet *decision*, write down *make a decision*.
2. **Notice them when you read.** Highlight verb + noun pairs in articles and stories.
3. **Use a learner's dictionary.** Good ones list the common partners of each word.
4. **Use them.** Write one sentence about your own life for each new chunk.

**Tip:** when a phrase feels odd, check it. Translating word for word from your first language is the most common source of unnatural collocations.
