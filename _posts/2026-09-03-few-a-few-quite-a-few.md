Some of the smallest words in English carry the biggest opinions. Compare:

> He has **few** close friends.
>
> He has **a few** close friends.

Both sentences describe a small number. But the first one sounds a little sad — almost none — while the second sounds perfectly fine — some, and that's enough. The difference is not about counting. It's about the *attitude* behind the count.

## The core system

| Word | Meaning | Feeling | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **few** | almost none | negative, disappointing | *Few students passed the exam.* |
| **a few** | some; a small number | positive or neutral | *A few students passed the exam.* |
| **quite a few** | many; more than expected | impressed | *Quite a few students passed — well done!* |

Notice how the whole mood of the sentence changes while the grammar barely moves:

- *She made **few** mistakes.* → focus on how little went wrong
- *She made **a few** mistakes.* → honest, but nobody is upset
- *She made **quite a few** mistakes.* → actually, quite a lot went wrong

## The uncountable twins: little and a little

For things you cannot count — water, time, money, patience — the family repeats itself exactly:

| Word | Meaning | Feeling | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **little** | almost none | negative | *We have little time.* (worrying) |
| **a little** | some; a small amount | positive | *We have a little time.* (workable) |
| **quite a little / quite a bit** | a fair amount | notable | *That's quite a bit of money.* |

## A trick worth stealing for interviews and emails

Because these words carry attitude, professionals use them deliberately:

> I have **few** complaints about the current system, but I do have **a few** suggestions.

Read that again: *few complaints* (almost none — good news) and *a few suggestions* (some — engaged, constructive). Same structure, opposite impressions. In one sentence you sound both satisfied and helpful.

## Two small traps

1. **"Only a few"** is different from "a few" — *only* drags the number down again: *There are only a few tickets left* means fewer than you would like.
2. **"Quite a few" means many**, not "a small number" — learners often mistake it for "very few". If 40 of 50 students passed, that is quite a few students.
