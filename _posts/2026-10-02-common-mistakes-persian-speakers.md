When you learn English through Persian, certain mistakes follow you from beginner to advanced levels. They are not signs of a bad student — they are signs of a *good* first language quietly applying its own rules to a new one.

Here are the ten I correct most often, with the fix and the reason behind it.

### 1. "I am agree"

- <span class="x">I am agree with you.</span>
- <span class="ok">I agree with you.</span>

In English, *agree* is a verb, not an adjective. No *am*, no *is* — just *I agree*.

### 2. "Explain me this"

- <span class="x">Can you explain me this word?</span>
- <span class="ok">Can you explain this word to me?</span>

*Explain* refuses to take a person as a direct object. The same goes for *describe*, *suggest*, and *say*: explain it *to* me, describe it *to* me.

### 3. "I went to shopping"

- <span class="x">I went to shopping yesterday.</span>
- <span class="ok">I went shopping yesterday.</span>

*Go* attaches directly to the *-ing* activity: go shopping, go swimming, go running. No *to*.

### 4. "Married with"

- <span class="x">He married with his classmate.</span>
- <span class="ok">He married his classmate.</span>
- <span class="ok">He is married to a classmate.</span>

The verb *marry* takes a direct object; the adjective *married* works with *to*.

### 5. "It depends of"

- <span class="x">It depends of the price.</span>
- <span class="ok">It depends on the price.</span>

*Depend* is loyal to one preposition: *on*. Always.

### 6. "One of my friend"

- <span class="x">One of my friend lives in Tabriz.</span>
- <span class="ok">One of my friends lives in Tabriz.</span>

*One of* is followed by a plural: one of my friends, one of the best books, one of those days.

### 7. "I have 25 years old"

- <span class="x">I have 25 years old.</span>
- <span class="ok">I am 25 years old.</span>

Persian says you *have* the years; English says you *are* them. Age works with the verb *be*.

### 8. "Informations" and "advices"

- <span class="x">She gave me some useful advices.</span>
- <span class="ok">She gave me some useful advice.</span>

*Advice*, *information*, *furniture*, and *homework* are uncountable in English — no plural *-s*, no *an*. Use *a piece of advice* or *some information*.

### 9. "How do you think?"

- <span class="x">How do you think about this idea?</span>
- <span class="ok">What do you think about this idea?</span>

*What* asks for the opinion itself; *how* asks about a manner or a method. Opinions are *what*.

### 10. "More better" and "very unique"

- <span class="x">This dictionary is more better.</span>
- <span class="ok">This dictionary is better.</span>
- <span class="x">Her pronunciation is very unique.</span>
- <span class="ok">Her pronunciation is unique.</span>

*Better* is already a comparative, so *more* adds nothing. And *unique* means one of a kind — something cannot be *very* one of a kind.
