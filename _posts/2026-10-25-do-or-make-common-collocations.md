Choosing between **do** and **make** can be confusing because many combinations are fixed. Learning them as chunks is more useful than translating each word.

## Five useful combinations

| Collocation | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **do homework** | I usually do my homework after dinner. |
| **do business** | The two companies do business together. |
| **make a decision** | We need to make a decision today. |
| **make a mistake** | Everyone makes mistakes when learning. |
| **make progress** | You're making good progress. |

## A simple way to practise

Don't study only the verbs *do* and *make*. Write down the complete expression and one sentence of your own. Then return to it later and try to recall it without looking.

## Speaking prompts

- What helps you make progress in English?
- Do you ever find it difficult to make a decision?
- What do you usually do when you make a mistake?

Try to include the collocations in your answers rather than saying them in isolation.
