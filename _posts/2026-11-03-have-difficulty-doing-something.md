Some English expressions are especially useful because they help you explain a problem naturally. One of them is **have difficulty doing something**.

## The basic pattern

Use this structure:

> **have difficulty + verb-ing**

- I have difficulty **understanding** fast speakers.
- She had difficulty **finding** the right words.
- We are having difficulty **making** a decision.

Notice that the verb after *difficulty* takes the **-ing** form, not the infinitive.

## Two useful alternatives

You can also say:

- **have trouble + verb-ing** — I'm having trouble logging in.
- **have a hard time + verb-ing** — He had a hard time getting used to his new job.

These expressions are close in meaning. *Have a hard time* is often a little more conversational.

You can use a noun after *difficulty* as well:

- We had difficulty **with the instructions**.
- She has trouble **with pronunciation**.

## Talk about your own experience

Complete these sentences with your own ideas:

1. I sometimes have difficulty...
2. I used to have trouble..., but now...
3. I would have a hard time... because...

The goal is not just to memorize the pattern. Try to use it when you explain a real challenge in your daily life.
