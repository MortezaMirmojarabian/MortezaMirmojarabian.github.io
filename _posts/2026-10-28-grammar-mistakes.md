We all make mistakes when learning a language, and that's completely fine — mistakes are how we learn. But some errors are so common that fixing them early gives you a huge payoff. Here are ten I see again and again in my students' writing.

<!--more-->

## 1. "He go to school every day."

Third-person singular **-s**: in the present simple, verbs take an **-s** with *he/she/it*.

- ❌ She **work** in a bank. → ✅ She **works** in a bank.

## 2. "I am agree."

*Agree* is a verb, so it doesn't need *to be*.

- ❌ I am agree with you. → ✅ I **agree** with you.

## 3. "I have 25 years old."

Age in English uses the verb *to be*, not *to have* (unlike many languages).

- ❌ I have 25 years old. → ✅ I **am** 25 **years old**.

## 4. "Informations"

Most English nouns are uncountable. *Information, advice, homework, news, furniture* and *research* have no plural form.

- ❌ I need some informations. → ✅ I need some **information**.

## 5. "Yesterday I have seen him."

When a specific finished time is mentioned (like *yesterday*), use the **past simple**, not the present perfect.

- ❌ I have seen him yesterday. → ✅ I **saw** him yesterday.

## 6. "Married with"

- ❌ She is married **with** a doctor. → ✅ She is married **to** a doctor.

## 7. "More better"

Comparatives are already comparative — don't double them.

- ❌ This one is more better. → ✅ This one is **better**.

## 8. "Despite of"

- ❌ Despite of the rain, we went out. → ✅ **Despite** the rain / **In spite of** the rain.

## 9. "Peoples"

*People* is already plural. *Peoples* exists but means "nations/ethnic groups" — a very different meaning.

- ❌ There were many peoples at the party. → ✅ There were many **people** at the party.

## 10. "Since five years"

With a *duration*, use **for**; with a *starting point*, use **since**.

- ✅ I've lived here **for** five years. / ✅ I've lived here **since** 2021.

---

Pick one or two of these, focus on them for a week, and you'll be surprised how quickly the correct forms become automatic.
