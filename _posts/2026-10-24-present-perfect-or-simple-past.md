Many learners know the forms of both tenses but still hesitate when they have to choose. This post gives you one question to ask yourself and a short list of signal words.

## One question to ask

**Is the time finished and clearly in the past, or does the situation connect to now?**

- Finished time: use the **simple past**. *I visited Isfahan in 2019.*
- Connection to now (or time that is not finished): use the **present perfect**. *I have visited Isfahan three times.*

## Signal words

| Usually simple past | Usually present perfect |
| --- | --- |
| yesterday | already |
| last week / month / year | yet |
| in 2019 | just |
| two days ago | ever / never |
| when I was a child | for / since |
| (any specific, finished time) | so far / recently / lately |

## A very common mistake

Because *yesterday* names a finished time, the present perfect does not fit:

I have seen him yesterday.
{: .no}

I saw him yesterday.
{: .ok}

## Same verb, different meaning

The tense changes what the listener understands:

- *I lived in Tehran for five years.* I don't live there now.
- *I have lived in Tehran for five years.* I still live there.
- *How long did you work there?* You don't work there any more.
- *How long have you worked here?* You still work here.
