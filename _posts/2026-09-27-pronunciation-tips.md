Good pronunciation is less about talent and more about **habit**. Here are five things that work for every learner I've coached.

<!--more-->

## 1. Learn the sounds, not the spelling

English spelling is unreliable. Learn words together with their sounds — use a dictionary with audio (Cambridge, Longman, or YouGlish for real-world examples) and repeat out loud.

## 2. Master word stress

Misplaced stress makes words hard to understand: *PHOtograph* vs *phoTOgraphy* vs *photoGRAPHic*. Whenever you learn a new word, learn its stress pattern too.

## 3. Use connected speech

Native speakers link words together: *"an apple"* sounds like *"a-napple"*, *"did you"* sounds like *"didja"*. Practise shadowing — repeat along with a short audio clip, copying the rhythm exactly.

## 4. Record yourself

Your phone is your best coach. Read a paragraph, record it, compare with a native version, and note one thing to fix. Do this twice a week.

## 5. Slow down

Fast, unclear speech is harder to understand than slow, clear speech. Clarity beats speed — always.

---

Ten focused minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Pick one tip and start today.
