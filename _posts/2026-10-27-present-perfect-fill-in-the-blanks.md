description: "Eight sentences to practice the present perfect with for, since, already, yet, ever and never."

exercise:
  kind: fill
  instructions: "Type the correct form of the verb in brackets. Short forms (I've) and full forms (I have) are both accepted."
  questions:
    - q: "I ___ (live) in Tehran since 2015."
      answer: ["have lived", "'ve lived"]
      explain: "*Since 2015* shows a situation that started in the past and continues now, so we use the present perfect."
    - q: "She ___ (not / finish) her homework yet."
      answer: ["has not finished", "hasn't finished"]
      explain: "*Yet* in a negative sentence goes with the present perfect: *has not (hasn't) finished*."
    - q: "Have you ever ___ (visit) Isfahan?"
      answer: "visited"
      explain: "*Ever* asks about experience up to now. After *have you ever*, we use the past participle."
    - q: "They ___ (already / eat) lunch."
      answer: ["have already eaten", "'ve already eaten"]
      explain: "*Already* signals the present perfect. Notice the word order: *have already eaten*."
    - q: "We ___ (know) each other for ten years."
      answer: ["have known", "'ve known"]
      explain: "*For ten years* shows a state that continues until now. *Know* is a state verb, so we do not say *have been knowing*."
    - q: "He ___ (lose) his keys, so he can't get in."
      answer: ["has lost", "'s lost"]
      explain: "The past event has a result now (he cannot get in), so we use the present perfect."
    - q: "How long has she ___ (work) here?"
      answer: "worked"
      explain: "*How long* + *has she* + past participle. She still works here."
    - q: "I ___ (never / see) such a beautiful sunset."
      answer: ["have never seen", "'ve never seen"]
      explain: "*Never* goes between *have* and the past participle. We use it for experiences in a lifetime."
---
The present perfect (*have / has* + past participle) connects the past with the present. We use it for experiences, for situations that continue until now, and for past events that matter now.
