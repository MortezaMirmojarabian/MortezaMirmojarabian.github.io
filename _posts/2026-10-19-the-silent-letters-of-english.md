Why is there a *b* in *debt* if nobody says it? English spelling is a museum of the language's history, and silent letters are its oldest exhibits. Here are the most important ones, with the patterns that hide them.

## Silent letters you meet every day

| Word | Silent letter | Word | Silent letter |
|------|---------------|------|---------------|
| knife | k | listen | t |
| write | w | island | s |
| hour | h | receipt | p |
| honest | h | castle | l |
| sign | g | doubt | b |

## The patterns

**Silent k and g before n:** *kn*- in *knee, knock, know*, and *gn*- in *gnaw, gnat*. Centuries ago English speakers pronounced both letters, then simplified. Spelling never caught up.

**Silent w before r:** *wrap, wrong, wrist, wreck, sword*.

**Silent h after w, or at the start of borrowed words:** *who, whole, whose*, and *honest, hour, heir* (from French, where the h was already silent).

**Silent t in -sten and -stle:** *listen, fasten, castle, whistle*. Only the *s* sound survives.

**Silent l after a vowel and consonant:** *walk, talk, half, could, would, calm*.

**Silent p and s:** *psychology, pneumonia* (Greek *ps-*), *island, aisle* (the s was actually added later, by mistake, to look more like *isle*, which came from French).

## Why it matters

- **Listening:** if you expect to hear "knee" with a k, you'll miss the word entirely. Expect the sound *nee*, and your brain connects it to the written form.
- **Speaking:** pronouncing silent letters is one of the fastest ways to sound unnatural. *Island* is *EYE-land*, not *is-land*.
- **Spelling:** knowing the pattern helps. *Receive* and *receipt* have silent p after ei; you don't hear it, but you know it belongs.

## Try this

Read this sentence aloud: *"The knight with the sword was honest, but his answer was wrong."* Half the consonants are silent: knight, sword, honest, answer, wrong. If you can say it smoothly, your pronunciation is ahead of most learners.
