English often uses **do** and **make** where other languages use a single verb. The useful distinction is not about effort; it is about what the action produces.

## Make something; do an activity

Use **make** when an action creates or produces a result: *make a plan, make a cup of tea, make a mistake,* or *make a decision*. Use **do** for work, tasks, and activities: *do your homework, do the dishes,* or *do some research*.

> We **made** a plan, then **did** the work.

A few common phrases do not follow a tidy rule, so notice them as whole units. We **do business**, **make progress**, **do someone a favor**, and **make a difference**.

## A quick way to check

Ask: “Is there a result being created?” If yes, **make** is often the natural choice. If you are naming a task or activity, try **do**. It is a helpful starting point—not a rule without exceptions.

Try making one example of each, then listen for these phrases in your next conversation or reading.
