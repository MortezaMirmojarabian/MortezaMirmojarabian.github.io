The expressions **be used to** and **get used to** look similar, but they describe different stages of becoming familiar with something.

## Be used to: something is familiar now

Use **be used to + noun / verb-ing** when something feels normal or familiar.

- I'm used to **working** under pressure.
- She isn't used to **the cold weather**.
- Are you used to **getting up early**?

In this pattern, *to* is a preposition, so a verb after it takes the **-ing** form.

## Get used to: the process of becoming familiar

Use **get used to + noun / verb-ing** to describe the process of adapting.

- I'm getting used to **my new schedule**.
- It took him a few weeks to get used to **driving on the left**.
- You'll get used to **speaking English every day**.

## Compare the two

- **I'm used to speaking in meetings.** Speaking in meetings is familiar to me.
- **I'm getting used to speaking in meetings.** I'm still adapting to it.

Remember: both patterns are followed by a noun or an **-ing** form. Avoid *I'm used to speak* and *I'm getting used to speak*.

## Make it personal

What is something you are used to doing now but found difficult in the past? What is something you are still getting used to? Try answering with the full patterns.
