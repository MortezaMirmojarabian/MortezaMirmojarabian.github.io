Some word pairs are so easily confused that mixing them up says nothing about your intelligence — and everything about how tricky English spelling is. Here are the pairs I correct most often.

## Affect / effect

- **Affect** (verb) = to influence: *Rain affects my mood.*
- **Effect** (noun) = the result: *The rain had a strange effect on my mood.*

Trick: **A** for **A**ction (affect), **E** for **E**nd result (effect).

## Its / it's

- **It's** = it is / it has: *It's raining again.*
- **Its** = possession: *The cat licked its paw.*

No, the apostrophe does not show possession here. English is like that sometimes.

## Your / you're

- **You're** = you are: *You're going to love this.*
- **Your** = possession: *Is this your book?*

## Then / than

- **Then** = time: *We ate, then we left.*
- **Than** = comparison: *She's taller than I am.*

## A quick reference table

| Confused pair | Quick test |
|---|---|
| affect / effect | Can you replace it with "influence"? → affect |
| its / it's | Can you expand to "it is"? → it's |
| your / you're | Can you expand to "you are"? → you're |
| then / than | Comparing? → than |

> When in doubt, read the sentence aloud with the expanded form ("it is", "you are"). Your ear catches what your eye misses.
