Native speakers don't talk like a textbook. They say things like *"I'm snowed under"* or *"that makes sense"* without thinking. Here are ten idioms you will genuinely hear and use in everyday English, with examples you can borrow.

## 1. Be snowed under

To have too much work.

> *"I'd love to meet for coffee, but I'm snowed under this week."*

## 2. Hit the books

To study hard.

> *"Exams start Monday, so it's time to hit the books."*

## 3. Get the hang of it

To learn how to do something with practice.

> *"Don't worry about the first driving lesson, you'll soon get the hang of it."*

## 4. Under the weather

Slightly ill.

> *"She's feeling a bit under the weather, so she stayed home today."*

## 5. Call it a day

To stop working on something.

> *"We've fixed the worst bugs, let's call it a day."*

## 6. Break the ice

To make people feel relaxed at the start of a meeting or conversation.

> *"He told a joke to break the ice at the interview."*

## 7. Piece of cake

Something very easy.

> *"The test was a piece of cake, I finished in ten minutes."*

## 8. On the same page

To agree about a situation or plan.

> *"Let's make sure everyone is on the same page before we start."*

## 9. Cut it short

To end something earlier than planned.

> *"I hate to cut it short, but I have a class at three."*

## 10. Get cold feet

To suddenly feel nervous about doing something you planned.

> *"He got cold feet right before the wedding speech."*

## A quick tip

Idioms are fixed: English speakers say *"a piece of cake"*, not *"a slice of cake"*, when they mean "easy". Learn the whole phrase, not word by word, and use one or two per conversation. That's what makes you sound natural, not the number of idioms you know.
