The present perfect links an earlier event to **now**. The form is *have* or *has* plus a past participle: *I have finished; she has seen it.*

## Experience, result, and duration

Use it to talk about experience without naming a finished time: *I’ve visited Tabriz twice.* Use it when a past action matters now: *I’ve lost my keys* (and I still need them). With **for** and **since**, it can describe a situation that continues: *We’ve worked together for three years.*

When you name a finished time—*yesterday, last week,* or *in 2022*—the simple past is usually the natural choice: *I visited Tabriz last year.*

## A small contrast

- *Have you read this article?* — at any point up to now
- *Did you read it yesterday?* — at a finished time

This is a guide to common patterns, not every use. Pay attention to whether the speaker is connecting the event to the present or placing it in a finished past time.
