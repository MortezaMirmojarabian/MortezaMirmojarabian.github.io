Few words cause learners as much trouble as the tiny ones. *A*, *an* and *the* appear in almost every English sentence, and choosing the wrong one — or adding one where none belongs — is one of the most common errors I see in my students' writing.

## The indefinite articles: a / an

Use **a** before consonant *sounds* and **an** before vowel *sounds* — sounds, not letters:

- a university, a European country (the sound is /j/)
- an hour, an honest answer (the *h* is silent)

Use them when you mention something for the first time, or when the exact identity doesn't matter: *I bought **a** laptop yesterday.*

## The definite article: the

Use **the** when both you and your reader know exactly which thing you mean:

- *I bought a laptop. **The** laptop was surprisingly cheap.* (now we know which one)
- ***The** sun*, ***the** internet*, ***the** government* — one of a kind, or clearly identified.

## The zero article

Sometimes the correct article is no article at all. With plural and uncountable nouns used in a general sense, leave it out:

- *Children love stories.* (children in general)
- *Life is easier with good coffee.* (life in general)

Compare: *The children in my class love stories.* — now we mean specific children.

## A quick checklist

1. First mention, countable, singular? → **a / an**
2. Both sides know which one? → **the**
3. General plural or uncountable? → **nothing**

> Articles are a habit, not a rulebook. Read a little every day and your ear will start rejecting the wrong choice before your brain explains why.
